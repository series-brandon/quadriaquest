# The dev playground

What each section of the **DEV PLAYGROUND** panel does (`npm run dev:debug`, then open the panel at the top of the game view). These notes used to sit inside the panel; they live here so the panel stays compact. The playground and everything in it is left out of normal builds (`npm run check:debug-isolation`). See also [README.md](../README.md#development-playground).

## Training systems

Portable production copper/stations/supplies/targets work in this map before visiting any tutorial. Add pickaxe/hammer and materials through Inventory. Use Combat for bow/Energy Strike. Move to cancel; reset to repeat. Crystal and station interface previews never grant items or teleport.

## Biome test

Every starter biome from [BIOMES.md](BIOMES.md), as bands of land 24 tiles deep from north to south: hills, pine forest, broadleaf forest, meadow, wetland and coast, then the sea. The land is 192 × 192 tiles, generated by `biome-land.js` from the rules in `biome-rules.js`, and streamed through the shared chunk streamer with the real fishing spots, enemies, resources, water and ground cover.
- **Go:** jumps to the chosen biome's band.
- **New seed:** regenerates the same bands with another seed.
- **What's here?:** names the biome, height and water under you.
- **Chunk outlines:** shows the streamed chunks.
- **Atmosphere** (BIOMES.md, *Atmosphere*): walking between bands crossfades each biome's fog and light over the haze every area has, and the minimap shows each biome's colour. `window.quadriaquest.getState().atmosphere` reports the biome, the fog density and whether fog is on.

## Streaming test

A generated 512 × 512 world streamed in 32 × 32 chunks (shared chunk streamer). Loads nearest first within a 4 ms per-frame budget, building each chunk in steps across frames, keeps one ring extra before unloading, and remembers depleted resources by id. Not listed on crystals; Back to the dream or a checkpoint leaves it.

## Travel practice

Uses shared travel and real crystals. Repeat trips, cancel during the fade, or reset the current area. Inventory and skills persist across travel.

## Tutorial checkpoints

Loads the selected area and tutorial with its prerequisites. Replaces the current test session. Cinderhold arrival/meet replay Bristle’s greeting and both responses; refused loads his flustered state. Visit the sarge landmark, then talk to leave or resume training. Reset current area to repeat.

Leaving the tutorial (production waking): Cinderhold finished or refused adds "???" to the crystal list. Wake up runs the farewell, reset, starter kit and arrival in the placeholder world; Back to the dream fully resets the session.

## Objective feedback

Quests tab: the list splits quests in progress from a collapsed Completed section; open one for its current objective and completed objectives, then All quests to go back. Start Playground Quest, open it, advance its parts (the pop-up names each part), finish it and check it moves to Completed. Finishing every current objective doesn't complete a quest until its story ends. Cues: progress (one note), a finished part (two), objective (three), quest (four); a re-worded lesson step is silent. Cues raised together play only the most important (level-up, quest, objective, part, progress): Objective + level-up together must play only the level-up. The Music & sound lab plays each cue alone.

## Maps

- **On start:** the playground opens in the world editor on the Quadria overworld, with the free camera on. A tool's options and what's selected show bottom left (in Decorate, above the catalogue); the map's settings and checks are under the *Map* tool group. Its *Map* list's *The clearing (where the game starts)* leaves the editor for the game's start (a fresh session). Add `?game` to the address to start in the game instead (the perf harness always does).
- **Open world editor:** opens on the map you're on. That's the map you were editing, left as it was, or the city you're standing in (you stay where you are), and otherwise the Quadria overworld. Its *Map* group opens any other map: the repo's maps (overworld first), the prop gallery, or a blank map. While editing, the world fills the window (Sims-style: tools along the top, a side panel, and the catalogue along the bottom in Decorate). *Play* is a test walk with the player interface back, where clicks move you as in the game. The editor's tools are described in docs/CITY.md (*Authoring*) and docs/TOWN.md.
- **World editor decorating** (docs/TOWN.md, *Sims-style editor* and *Colours*):
  - Decorate: Deco is the catalogue (by function or room, with search): pick a thing to hold it; the Hand lifts and puts down, with all of a selection's options; `,` and `.` turn; Shift-click places more; Esc puts it back.
  - Colours: any colour for each piece, quick swatches, and presets for an item or a scheme for anything (`src/palettes/`).
  - Eyedropper copies, colours and all; Sledgehammer removes.
  - Indoors (docs/TOWN.md, *Decorating indoors, doors and stairs* and *Wall and floor paint*): Walls up / Cutaway / Walls down and ▲ ▼ (Page Up / Down) in the bar; Floor paint and Wall paint; the Hand picks up doors and stairs; the catalogue furnishes ground floors in place.
  - Build: Room (drag a rectangle) and Push/pull walls (outside walls, and the walls of rooms you drew). The Hand also moves a room's door to another of its walls.
  - Ghosts and walls (docs/TOWN.md, *Ghosts, aiming at walls, floor patterns, sizing and names*): whatever is placed or held shows as a ghost, buildings included. Walls, Wall paint and wall decorations (e.g. Painting, Wall torch) follow the wall face under the pointer.
  - Floor paint's Pattern and Intensity; `+` / `−` (Bigger / Smaller) size a held or selected prop; a selected building's Name is in its Look card.
- **World editor drags and stamps** (docs/TOWN.md, *Box select, copy and stamps* and *Lines and brushes*):
  - Select: drag a box to select a group. Ctrl/⌘+C and Ctrl/⌘+V copy and paste.
  - Stamp: save a selection and place it again (`src/stamps/`).
  - Edge: drag to lay a fence line. Ramp: drag a slope.
  - Smooth, brush sizes up to 9 × 9, a straight-line brush shape, and water in Surface.
- **Prop gallery:** every prop by category, with their live interactions (sit, sleep, cook, smith, open, read), a fence gate, a low stone wall and an iron gate.
- **Force cutaway:** cuts every building at once (no roofs, one-storey walls).
- **Ramps** (docs/TOWN.md, *Ramps*): *Terrain* → *Ramp ledges* turns ledges under the brush into ramps (paths only by default) or back; *Play* walks them smoothly. The old *Ramp* drag tool is now *Slope*.
- **Townsfolk** (docs/TOWN.md, *The Townsfolk and Crystal tools' panels*): the Townsfolk tool places someone and opens their panel; clicking someone there opens theirs.
- **Iter Crystals** (docs/TOWN.md, *Iter Crystals*): the editor's *World* → *Crystal* places any number; the Hand's panel names them and sets their id and *Known from the start*. In *Play*, walking up to one discovers it for the teleport list. The debug panel's *Maps* has *Discover all crystals* and *Forget crystals*.
- **Play from where you work:** *Play* puts the slime on the open tile nearest the view and gives the camera back to it, as in the game; *Edit* frees the camera again. *Player here* (*Map* → *View*) moves the slime there without leaving Edit. The debug panel's *Maps* has *Player to camera* for the same outside the editor.
- **Save** (top row, or Ctrl/⌘+S): saves the map to its file in `src/maps`, as *Map* → *Save to repo*; it shows a dot while there are unsaved changes.
- **Draw distance:** the world editor's top row has it too (*Draw*), the same setting as Settings.
- **The world's edge and atmosphere** (docs/TOWN.md, *The mist wall*; BIOMES.md, *Atmosphere*): the *Map* group's *View* has *Atmosphere* (biome fog and light on or off; the haze stays) and *Show the mist wall* (the camera looks at the edge nearest the view). In *Play*, clicking past the edge says the mist is too thick. Gelton's countryside (*Enter Gelton*) has the mist round it too.
- **Reworked props** (docs/TOWN.md, *Reworked props*): the stall, counters, lamps, crates, barrels, fences, gates and trees show in the catalogue, the model preview and anywhere they're placed.
- **Free camera** (also a switch in the world editor):
  - the camera leaves the slime;
  - pan with WASD (Shift: faster) or a middle-button drag, which grabs the ground; right-drag and the arrow keys still orbit;
  - zoom out to 140;
  - the land, towns and the sun's shadows follow the camera, so you can fly anywhere and edit there;
  - turning it off brings the camera back to the slime.

  The editor's shortcuts never take WASD or the arrow keys.
- **Removed (2026-10-14):** the graybox and kit street buttons, Enter Gelton, the crowd, roof, cutaway, door and zoom buttons. The kit street lives on only as a test street for tests and the `city-walk` and `city-crowd` perf scenarios (`src/dev/kit-street.js`). Gelton is still reached by the Countryside and city-greens jumps.

## Walls & doors

Walls, windows, fences, railings, counters and doors sit on tile edges (shared rules in world.js; doors.js). Walls block walking, sight and reach; windows and fences let sight through; a counter lets you talk and strike across it; a closed door blocks like a wall until someone opens it (click it: you walk up and open it; click again to close); open, it swings into its own building and lies there as a wall; a locked one won't open and rattles. Every door needs a wall on each side before a corner (the door rule). The building test map has a small house (one door), a large hall (two doors), a complex building with rooms (each with its own door), and along the southern road an L, an L split into two buildings by a wall, a T, a U with a courtyard, a + and an S/Z. Build the yard in any map, open the door, walk in, lock it, and spawn enemies (Combat practice) to see them route around walls. The edge overlay colours edges by kind (locked doors magenta).

## Walk-through characters

Living characters (every slime, creatures of any size, the companion) never block movement; they mark their tile occupied. Occupied tiles cost extra (a player's far more, so NPCs almost never cross one): routes go around a character when the detour is short and pass through otherwise. A player who stops on a character pushes it to a free neighbouring tile; characters set to stay put (tutorial NPCs) hide under the player instead. Pushed NPCs return home; wandering creatures pushed out of their area walk back (far away, they reappear at home). Solid objects (trees, rocks, stations, practice targets, walls) block. Spawn the crowd or the passive golem on a clear lane ahead, then walk through it (the companion follows if you have one). The overlay marks solid tiles red and tiles where a character stands blue. Works in any map; Remove clears both fixtures.

## NPC slimes

Every slime (player, mentors, Reed) renders through the shared slime presentation. Make an NPC play any shared motion in the world with its own gear (Fletch keeps his bow). Mentors bring you to them; Reed needs Willowbank. Stop returns it to idle.

## Movement

Walkers step diagonally where a corner is clear (docs/MOVEMENT-PLAN.md): both tiles beside the diagonal must be open and level, with nothing on the four edges around the corner, so nobody cuts past a rock, water, a wall end or a doorway. A diagonal step takes as long as a straight one. Hops and stairs stay straight.

- **Diagonal steps** switches diagonals on or off per walker group (player, companion, NPCs, townsfolk, creatures, large bodies like the golem). Each flips the real setting, so you can compare routes with and without.
- **Melee reach** and **Range metric** tune combat geometry on the real combat code: melee reaches the four cardinal tiles (default) or all eight; attack range, aggro and leash count Manhattan tiles (default), Chebyshev or Euclidean. Spawn Combat practice enemies to try them. Both are session-only.
- **Corner yard** builds free-standing edge pieces near you on an open, level 7×4 patch: a two-tile wall, a fence and a window with free ends, a counter, and a doorway (an open door between walls). Walk past their ends and through the doorway: routes go diagonal only round clear corners and pass the doorway straight on. The clearing and Willowbank are too cramped; use the building test map, Gelton or the overworld.
- **Show routes** draws the routes about to be walked: the player (blue), the companion (orange) and creatures pursuing, returning home or wandering (red).

Solid corners, water corners and ledges are in the clearing (trees, the pond, the raised meadow).

## Companions

Shared follower controls work in every area. Doze off / Wake up tests sleep with the player.

The companion follows a little slower than you walk (Pace, a share of your walking speed with Celerity and Rush) and twice that while you sprint, so it is never faster than you. It stays put while it is beside you (any of the 8 tiles), starts once you are **Start at** tiles away, and walks straight through people, you included, re-planning at every tile toward where you are now. It never stops on you or your next tile. Further than **Teleport past** tiles, round a long detour, with no way to you (a locked door, a ledge), on a new map, or when you move at once, it pops in beside you, behind you when it can. Teleport to me runs that pop now; Send 2, 5 or 9 tiles away places it behind you to watch it catch up (or teleport). Show follow state reports its state, speed and last teleport reason. The tuning inputs are session-only; Default follow tuning restores them. Your own routes ignore your companion; NPCs and creatures still step around it.

## Terrain colors

Live clearing and splash grass color. Subtle tile variations stay centered on your chosen color. Session-only; reset restores the original palette.

## Water animation

Short drifting shimmer lines in the clearing and splash ponds. Compare surface waves and faceted lighting independently. Wave strength 4 is the default. Uncheck Fixed shoreline to let waves lap against banks. Lower roughness gives sharper highlights; reflection strength 0 removes sky reflections. Opacity 1 is opaque; lower it to reveal the shallow bed and pebbles. Speed 0 pauses; Water intensity controls only the shimmer strips and has no effect while they are off.

## Skills

Combat skills and every proficiency use the adopted XP curve and route through the production award (core XP converts 5:1; each core level grants 3 attribute points). Prototype skills keep 120 XP per level.

## Character & combat profile

Uses the production character. Set an attribute's total value (base 10 for capacity attributes, 1 otherwise; unspent points adjust), grant points, then spend them with + in Skills. Constitution/Mentis/Endurance change maximum Health/Mana/Stamina without refilling. Strategy and spell selection live in the real Combat menu. Show combat profile prints the attack the next windup would commit, plus your current defenses. Full test area resets the character.

Abilities and auras use the production queue and Ki system (no mentor yet: learn here). Strong Strike queues for the next melee attack (HUD lightning button or Combat menu; press again to withdraw), spends 50 Energy on impact and cancels if Energy drops below 50 before then. Toggle Rush/Harden in Combat: 0.5 Ki fee + 0.5 Ki/s each; set Ki low with Player health controls to watch exhaustion turn both off, then Ki recover. Eat mid-fight: healing applies at once, the windup restarts, the queued ability clears and a second meal waits for the 2s cooldown. At a real crystal (Travel practice → Walk to crystal) use Restore (keeps auras) and Redistribute (refunds points, turns auras off), both outside combat only.

## Assistance, dual wield, armor, control & backfire

All production systems. **Assistance**: the Combat menu's Auto/Manual, Pacifist, Class (runs Optimize), training goal and Advanced settings; Auto reads actual enemy stats for warnings (HUD status), adaptive retaliation (withheld at 1–3 hits unless you attacked that target), auto-eat at ≤150% of the max hit and auto auras. Lower health or fight a Bruiser to see bands; Show assistance state prints the assessment. **Hands**: any one-handed item (shields too) fits either hand, and a bow can be held in either. Add a second Copper Dagger via Inventory, equip the right and left hands from Equipment, then set Dominant hand, Attack hands and per-hand damage types in Combat → Attack setup. The model viewer previews every combination, left-handed included. **Armor**: add test pieces (Medium/Heavy ones need levels 10/20 to be fully effective), wear them, take hits for armor XP. **Control**: test stuns/slows on you or the nearest enemy; protection windows show in the HUD status and enemy label. **Backfire**: raise Energy Strike's Magic Technique requirement, then cast (Combat shows the risk). Full test area resets all of these.

## Drops & jobs

Jobs (jobs.js): noticeboards are job boards; in Gelton, Pim of the Post at the square has deliveries, and the smiths, fishmongers and bakers with a gold "!" over their heads have jobs. Stand-in givers and a stand-in recipient work in any map; Finish jobs supplies or counts what each needs.

## Combat practice

Spawn portable enemies in the current area. Pacifist (Attacks: Prevented) draws no aggression: spawn aggressive enemies, pick Pacifist on the Combat page and walk past them; pacifist-hunting enemies still engage (their hover reads “Hunts pacifists”). Switching to Pacifist mid-chase keeps that enemy aggressive until the leash or a safe tile ends it. Enemy HP, damage bounds, attack interval and Threat derive from shared stat sheets through the production combat formulas (Scrapper 50 HP, 1–10; Bruiser 100 HP, 3–20; both every 2.5s). Show enemy stat sheets prints the derived values; the table lists every configured input. Reset restores these shared baselines. Fight unarmed or craft/equip gear through the normal menus; every attack resolves through the shared formulas and awards per-attack XP (strategy skill + weapon proficiency; Shield proficiency when hit or blocking with a shield). Splats distinguish Miss!, Dodged!, Blocked! and orange criticals. Choose strategies in the real Combat menu, including mid-fight; the next attack uses them. Walk away to exercise pursuit and leash; use health controls and Eat during combat. Toggle Auto-Retaliate in the real Combat menu, including while fighting. Passive enemies ignore proximity; aggressive fixtures notice within 3 tiles (outside safe zones). Test crafting/fishing interruption, combat while eating, and first melee/ranged/magic impacts including misses. Reset restores enemies; Full test area removes fixtures and resets equipment/XP. The model viewer has goblin, combat, and defeat motions. Enemy attacks commit halfway through their windup: leave reach in the first half to cancel; after that a normal attack lands wherever you are. Walking into an enemy keeps its windup going. The Cinder Golem spawn is the real boss (slow walk, hunts pacifists, no XP): its slam locks the 3×3 tiles around you at the halfway point (red ground markers) and hits up to 450 there, so get off them; stay out of its reach for 1.5 s and it throws a rock at the 3×3 around you. Swap weapons mid-fight through Inventory/Equipment: a hand swap interrupts the unlanded attack, and the next lands on the previous weapon's timer or the new windup, whichever is later. Its arena version with Fletch’s gate and the Bristle/Borin questions loads from the Cinderhold boss checkpoints. This fixture adds no quest.

## Player health
