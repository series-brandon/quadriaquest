# The dev playground

What each section of the **DEV PLAYGROUND** panel does (`npm run dev:debug`, then open the panel at the top of the game view). These notes used to sit inside the panel; they live here so the panel stays compact. The playground and everything in it is left out of normal builds (`npm run check:debug-isolation`). See also [README.md](../README.md#development-playground).

## Training systems

Portable production copper/stations/supplies/targets work in this map before visiting any tutorial. Add pickaxe/hammer and materials through Inventory. Use Combat for bow/Energy Strike. Move to cancel; reset to repeat. Crystal and station interface previews never grant items or teleport.

## Biome test

Every starter biome from [BIOMES.md](BIOMES.md), as bands of land 24 tiles deep from north to south: hills, pine forest, broadleaf forest, meadow, wetland and coast, then the sea. The land is 192 × 192 tiles, generated by `biome-land.js` from the rules in `biome-rules.js`, and streamed through the shared chunk streamer with the real fishing spots, enemies, resources, water and ground cover.
- **Go:** jumps to the chosen biome's band.
- **New seed:** regenerates the same bands with another seed.
- **What's here?:** names the biome, height and water under you.
- **Chunk outlines:** shows the streamed chunks.
- **Atmosphere** (BIOMES.md, *Atmosphere*): walking between bands crossfades each biome's fog and light over the haze every area has, and the minimap shows each biome's colour. `window.quadriaquest.getState().atmosphere` reports the biome, the fog density and whether fog is on.

## Streaming test

A generated 512 × 512 world streamed in 32 × 32 chunks (shared chunk streamer). Loads nearest first within a 4 ms per-frame budget, building each chunk in steps across frames, keeps one ring extra before unloading, and remembers depleted resources by id. Not listed on crystals; Back to the dream or a checkpoint leaves it.

## Travel practice

Uses shared travel and real crystals. Repeat trips, cancel during the fade, or reset the current area. Inventory and skills persist across travel.

## Tutorial checkpoints

Loads the selected area and tutorial with its prerequisites. Replaces the current test session. Cinderhold arrival/meet replay Bristle’s greeting and both responses; refused loads his flustered state. Visit the sarge landmark, then talk to leave or resume training. Reset current area to repeat.

Leaving the tutorial (production waking): Cinderhold finished or refused adds "???" to the crystal list. Wake up runs the farewell, reset, starter kit and arrival in the placeholder world; Back to the dream fully resets the session.

## Objective feedback

Quests tab: the list splits quests in progress from a collapsed Completed section; open one for its current objective and completed objectives, then All quests to go back. Start Playground Quest, open it, advance its parts (the pop-up names each part), finish it and check it moves to Completed. Finishing every current objective doesn't complete a quest until its story ends. Cues: progress (one note), a finished part (two), objective (three), quest (four); a re-worded lesson step is silent. Cues raised together play only the most important (level-up, quest, objective, part, progress): Objective + level-up together must play only the level-up. The Music & sound lab plays each cue alone.

## Walls & doors

Walls, windows, fences, railings, counters and doors sit on tile edges (shared rules in world.js; doors.js). Walls block walking, sight and reach; windows and fences let sight through; a counter lets you talk and strike across it; a closed door blocks like a wall until someone opens it (click it: you walk up and open it; click again to close); open, it swings into its own building and lies there as a wall; a locked one won't open and rattles. Every door needs a wall on each side before a corner (the door rule). The building test map has a small house (one door), a large hall (two doors), a complex building with rooms (each with its own door), and along the southern road an L, an L split into two buildings by a wall, a T, a U with a courtyard, a + and an S/Z. Build the yard in any map, open the door, walk in, lock it, and spawn enemies (Combat practice) to see them route around walls. The edge overlay colours edges by kind (locked doors magenta).

## Walk-through characters

Living characters (every slime, creatures of any size, the companion) never block movement; they mark their tile occupied. Occupied tiles cost extra (a player's far more, so NPCs almost never cross one): routes go around a character when the detour is short and pass through otherwise. A player who stops on a character pushes it to a free neighbouring tile; characters set to stay put (tutorial NPCs) hide under the player instead. Pushed NPCs return home; wandering creatures pushed out of their area walk back (far away, they reappear at home). Solid objects (trees, rocks, stations, practice targets, walls) block. Spawn the crowd or the passive golem on a clear lane ahead, then walk through it (the companion follows if you have one). The overlay marks solid tiles red and tiles where a character stands blue. Works in any map; Remove clears both fixtures.

## NPC slimes

Every slime (player, mentors, Reed) renders through the shared slime presentation. Make an NPC play any shared motion in the world with its own gear (Fletch keeps his bow). Mentors bring you to them; Reed needs Willowbank. Stop returns it to idle.

## Movement

Walkers step diagonally where a corner is clear (docs/MOVEMENT-PLAN.md): both tiles beside the diagonal must be open and level, with nothing on the four edges around the corner, so nobody cuts past a rock, water, a wall end or a doorway. A diagonal step takes as long as a straight one. Hops and stairs stay straight.

- **Diagonal steps** switches diagonals on or off per walker group (player, companion, NPCs, townsfolk, creatures, large bodies like the golem). Each flips the real setting, so you can compare routes with and without.
- **Melee reach** and **Range metric** tune combat geometry on the real combat code: melee reaches the four cardinal tiles (default) or all eight; attack range, aggro and leash count Manhattan tiles (default), Chebyshev or Euclidean. Spawn Combat practice enemies to try them. Both are session-only.
- **Corner yard** builds free-standing edge pieces near you on an open, level 7×4 patch: a two-tile wall, a fence and a window with free ends, a counter, and a doorway (an open door between walls). Walk past their ends and through the doorway: routes go diagonal only round clear corners and pass the doorway straight on. The clearing and Willowbank are too cramped; use the building test map, Gelton or the overworld.
- **Show routes and patrol areas** draws the routes about to be walked: the player (blue), the companion (orange) and creatures pursuing, returning home or wandering (red). Each creature's patrol area (the square it wanders in while calm: its spawn's or type's *Patrol radius*) is outlined in yellow; change the radius in the NPCs tool and the outline follows.

Solid corners, water corners and ledges are in the clearing (trees, the pond, the raised meadow).

## Companions

Shared follower controls work in every area. Doze off / Wake up tests sleep with the player.

The companion follows a little slower than you walk (Pace, a share of your walking speed with Celerity and Rush) and twice that while you sprint, so it is never faster than you. It stays put while it is beside you (any of the 8 tiles), starts once you are **Start at** tiles away, and walks straight through people, you included, re-planning at every tile toward where you are now. It never stops on you or your next tile. Further than **Teleport past** tiles, round a long detour, with no way to you (a locked door, a ledge), on a new map, or when you move at once, it pops in beside you, behind you when it can. Teleport to me runs that pop now; Send 2, 5 or 9 tiles away places it behind you to watch it catch up (or teleport). Show follow state reports its state, speed and last teleport reason. The tuning inputs are session-only; Default follow tuning restores them. Your own routes ignore your companion; NPCs and creatures still step around it.

## Terrain colors

Live clearing and splash grass color. Subtle tile variations stay centered on your chosen color. Session-only; reset restores the original palette.

## Water animation

Short drifting shimmer lines in the clearing and splash ponds. Compare surface waves and faceted lighting independently. Wave strength 4 is the default. Uncheck Fixed shoreline to let waves lap against banks. Lower roughness gives sharper highlights; reflection strength 0 removes sky reflections. Opacity 1 is opaque; lower it to reveal the shallow bed and pebbles. Speed 0 pauses; Water intensity controls only the shimmer strips and has no effect while they are off.

## Skills

Every skill and proficiency uses the adopted XP curve and routes through the production award (core XP converts 5:1; each core level grants 3 attribute points). Pacing rules are in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md#skill-tiers-and-pacing).

## Character & combat profile

Uses the production character. Set an attribute's total value (base 10 for capacity attributes, 1 otherwise; unspent points adjust), grant points, then spend them with + in Skills. Constitution/Mentis/Endurance change maximum Health/Mana/Stamina without refilling. Strategy and spell selection live in the real Combat menu. Show combat profile prints the attack the next windup would commit, plus your current defenses. Full test area resets the character.

Abilities and auras use the production queue and Ki system (no mentor yet: learn here). Strong Strike queues for the next melee attack (HUD lightning button or Combat menu; press again to withdraw), spends 50 Energy on impact and cancels if Energy drops below 50 before then. Toggle Rush/Harden in Combat: 0.5 Ki fee + 0.5 Ki/s each; set Ki low with Player health controls to watch exhaustion turn both off, then Ki recover. Eat mid-fight: healing applies at once, the windup restarts, the queued ability clears and a second meal waits for the 2s cooldown. At a real crystal (Travel practice → Walk to crystal) use Restore (keeps auras) and Redistribute (refunds points, turns auras off), both outside combat only.

## Assistance, dual wield, armor, control & backfire

All production systems. **Assistance**: the Combat menu's Auto/Manual, Pacifist, Class (runs Optimize), training goal and Advanced settings; Auto reads actual enemy stats for warnings (HUD status), adaptive retaliation (withheld at 1–3 hits unless you attacked that target), auto-eat at ≤150% of the max hit and auto auras. Lower health or fight a Bruiser to see bands; Show assistance state prints the assessment. **Hands**: any one-handed item (shields too) fits either hand, and a bow can be held in either. Add a second Copper Dagger via Inventory, equip the right and left hands from Equipment, then set Dominant hand, Attack hands and per-hand damage types in Combat → Attack setup. The model viewer previews every combination, left-handed included. **Armor**: add test pieces (Medium/Heavy ones need levels 10/20 to be fully effective), wear them, take hits for armor XP. **Crude gear**: Inventory → *Add crude gear* gives one Crude Dagger, Crude Sword, Crude Shield and Crude Helm (real items, the weakest tier; the helm is light Head armor, so *Add all test armor* adds it too). Equip them from Equipment (the daggers stab or slash by damage type, the sword slashes), fight a practice enemy, and take hits in the helm for Light Armor and Helm XP. Crafting makes all four from Sticks and Rocks; the Weaponsmith and Armourer stock them. The model viewer holds the dagger and sword in either hand and wears the helm in the Head slot. **Control**: test stuns/slows on you or the nearest enemy; protection windows show in the HUD status and enemy label. **Backfire**: raise Energy Strike's Magic Technique requirement, then cast (Combat shows the risk). Full test area resets all of these.

## Combat practice

Spawn portable enemies in the current area: passive, *as their types* (each type's own aggression: it attacks only while your Threat is below its *Aggressive until Threat*), or aggressive. *Threat and aggression* prints your Threat and, for each enemy here, whether it is aggressive to you, its threshold and its next planned attack. *Force attack* makes the practice enemies (and any enemy you are fighting) use only the chosen attack, regular or special, so any special can be tried on any enemy; *Type's mix* returns them to their own roll. *Creature* spawns any enemy type beside you as its type (Threat shown in the list); *Fight* walks you to it. Point at it to see its hover: "Attack <name>" over "Level N", green, orange or red against your Threat; change your Threat with *Set attribute* (Character & combat profile) and point again. The wild creatures (vole, squirrel, rat, crab, spider, scorpion) also stream into biome land from each biome's table (Biome test), and the model viewer shows each with Idle, Walk, Attack and Hit. Raise your Threat (Character & combat profile: Set attribute; Skills) or change the type's threshold (NPCs tool) to cross the line. Pacifist (Attacks: Prevented) draws no aggression: spawn aggressive enemies, pick Pacifist on the Combat page and walk past them; pacifist-hunting enemies still engage (their hover reads “Hunts pacifists”). Switching to Pacifist mid-chase keeps that enemy aggressive until the leash or a safe tile ends it. Enemy HP, damage bounds, attack interval and Threat derive from shared stat sheets through the production combat formulas (Scrapper 50 HP, 1–10; Bruiser 100 HP, 3–20; both every 2.5s). Show enemy stat sheets prints the derived values; the table lists every configured input. Reset restores these shared baselines. Fight unarmed or craft/equip gear through the normal menus; every attack resolves through the shared formulas and awards per-attack XP (strategy skill + weapon proficiency; Shield proficiency when hit or blocking with a shield). Splats distinguish Miss!, Dodged!, Blocked! and orange criticals. Choose strategies in the real Combat menu, including mid-fight; the next attack uses them. Walk away to exercise pursuit and leash; use health controls and Eat during combat. Toggle Auto-Retaliate in the real Combat menu, including while fighting. Passive enemies ignore proximity; aggressive fixtures notice within 3 tiles (outside safe zones). Test crafting/fishing interruption, combat while eating, and first melee/ranged/magic impacts including misses. Reset restores enemies; Full test area removes fixtures and resets equipment/XP. The model viewer has goblin, combat, and defeat motions. Enemy attacks commit halfway through their windup: leave reach in the first half to cancel; after that a normal attack lands wherever you are. Walking into an enemy keeps its windup going. The Cinder Golem spawn is the real boss (slow walk, hunts pacifists, no XP): its slam locks the 3×3 tiles around you at the halfway point (red ground markers) and hits up to 450 there, so get off them; stay out of its reach for 1.5 s and it throws a rock at the 3×3 around you. Swap weapons mid-fight through Inventory/Equipment: a hand swap interrupts the unlanded attack, and the next lands on the previous weapon's timer or the new windup, whichever is later. Its arena version with Fletch’s gate and the Bristle/Borin questions loads from the Cinderhold boss checkpoints. This fixture adds no quest.

## Lumberjack practice

Plants production trees next to the player in whichever map you are in (planting after travel starts over in the new map).
- **Tree** picks a tier. **Plant this tree** plants one; **Plant every tier** plants one of each, lowest tier nearest, as far as nearby free tiles allow.
- **Set Lumberjack to its level** sets the skill exactly to the tree's requirement, so you can try it one level under (use Skills to lower it) and at its level.
- **Chop time and XP** reads the chop time for your level and best usable axe, the XP per tree, its sure drop and yield bonus, and the regrow time.
- **Fell planted trees (stumps)** turns them all into stumps that regrow on the normal timer; **Regrow** resets them at once; **Remove** clears them.
- **Give axe** adds an axe tier to the inventory. Only the Crude Axe exists for now; new tiers appear here automatically from `tool-tiers.js`.

Clicking a tree above your level shows the level it needs, in the toast and the hover text. Each tier's model, with its hit and fall, is in the model viewer as *Tree (… · Lumberjack N)*, its stump as *Stump (…)*, and the boulder and copper remnants as *Rubble (…)*.

## Resource picking

Trees, boulders, and ground items in both areas use shared actions. Inventory supplies axes and pickaxes; Skills adjusts Gathering, Lumberjack, and Mining. Move to cancel before depletion. Ground items, Trees, Boulders, and Full test area resets support repeats in the clearing. Trees regrow on their tier's timer (Poplars, as in the clearing and Willowbank, in 10 seconds); other resources respawn after 8. A felled tree or mined rock leaves its stump or rubble on a still-blocked tile, so regrowth is never delayed. Load Willowbank · Collect Flint to check its narrative callback. Model viewer motions reuse the production hit/depletion animations.

## Performance

Same probe and scenarios as `npm run perf`. The HUD shows main-thread frame time, GPU time when the browser exposes it, draw calls, scene objects and the slowest loop phases. Prepare loads a perf scenario with its fixed camera; census logs scene contents by group and mesh kind to the console.

**Density step** builds one step of the `view-density` sweep (docs/PERFORMANCE.md, *Density*) round the clearing walk: that many real props, each its own mesh (`separate`) or merged per quarter as town decoration is (`merged`). Prepare `view-density` first: it starts the walk (shadows redraw every frame, as when playing) and pins the graphics tier Auto picked, so the tier can't step down mid-sweep; preparing another scenario removes the props and restores the tier. Read draws, triangles and frame time off the perf HUD at each step; step 0 (no props) clears them.

## Music lab

Arrangement: Cozy plays the Stardew-style versions (every song has one); Original plays the first versions. Switching crossfades.

Style picks which parts of the song play: written themes, generated drift, or both. It restarts the song.

Combat layer: hand drums and a bass pulse that fade in while fighting (Follow uses the real combat state).

Instruments: play one through the music mix. Click checks render offline, dry, and flag waveform jumps; songs are checked layer by layer (percussion is noise and is skipped).

Effects: cues (marked ♪) play on the current song's cue instrument in its key, so try them over different songs. Progress plays at 50%.

Ambience follows the place: a breeze on the splash, silence in the opening scene, then each area's profile. Birds go quiet during fights.
